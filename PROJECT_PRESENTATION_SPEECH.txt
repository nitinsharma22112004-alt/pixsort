# PART 2 — AI & VECTOR SEARCH

### Speaker: Palak

**"Thank you, Om.**

Good morning Sir.

I worked mainly on the **AI and vector search part** of PixSort.

When a user uploads a photo, our system processes it in two main steps.

### Step 1 — Understanding the Image

First, we use **Google Gemini 2.5 Flash** to understand the image.

Gemini looks at the image and identifies things such as:

* People
* Objects
* Places
* Activities
* Text
* Lighting
* Mood

For example, if we upload a photo of friends having dinner, AI can understand that it contains **friends, food, a dinner table, and a happy environment**.

### Step 2 — Creating a Vector

After understanding the image, we convert this information into a **3072-dimensional vector** using Google's **Gemini Embedding 2** model.

Now, what is a vector?

We can think of a vector as a **mathematical representation of the meaning of an image**.

For example:

A photo of a dog and a photo of a puppy will have similar vector positions because their meanings are similar.

Similarly, a photo of a beach and a photo of an ocean will also be close to each other.

When the user searches something like:

**'happy dinner with friends'**

we also convert the search text into a vector.

Then we compare the search vector with our photo vectors.

For this, we use **Cosine Similarity**.

The formula is:

**Cosine Similarity = A · B / (|A| × |B|)**

Since our vectors are normalized, this becomes basically a **dot product**.

A higher similarity score means the photo is more related to the search.

### Local Search

One important feature is that we don't need to call Gemini every time the user searches.

We create the vectors **once when the photo is uploaded**.

After that, the vectors are kept in our local memory.

So when the user searches, our system compares the vectors locally.

This makes the search very fast and also reduces API usage.

Now I will hand over to **Nitin**, who will explain our frontend and user experience."

# PART 4 — STORAGE, SECURITY & CONCLUSION

### Speaker: Om

**"Thank you, Nitin.**

Now I will explain two important parts of our project: **Google Drive storage pooling and security**.

## 1. Multiple Google Drive Accounts

A user can connect more than one Google Drive account to PixSort.

For example:

* Drive A — 15 GB
* Drive B — 15 GB

Our system keeps these accounts in a priority order.

When the user uploads a photo:

**First, we try Drive A.**

If Drive A does not have enough space, we automatically try **Drive B**.

So the user does not have to manually select where each photo should go.

Users can also change the priority or disconnect an account from the storage pool.

Importantly, disconnecting an account from PixSort does **not delete the user's original files from Google Drive**.

---

## 2. Security

Security is also an important part of our project.

We support **Google Sign-In** and **Email/Password login**.

For Google Drive access, we use the **drive.file permission**.

This limits our application's access to files created or managed by PixSort rather than giving access to the user's entire Drive.

We also keep every user's photo data separate.

For example:

**User A should only be able to access User A's photos.**

We enforce this using **Firebase Authentication and Firestore Security Rules**.

---

## Final Summary

So, our project provides:

* **AI-based photo understanding**
* **Natural language photo search**
* **Vector-based similarity search**
* **Fast local searching**
* **Google Drive storage**
* **Multiple Drive account support**
* **Smart albums without duplicate files**
* **User authentication and data isolation**

The main idea of PixSort is simple:

**Instead of searching for a photo by its filename, search for it by what you remember about the photo.**

Now we would like to demonstrate our application."

# PART 6 — VIVA QUESTIONS

## Q1. Why did you use Google Drive instead of AWS S3?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, our main reason was cost and ownership. With Google Drive, the user's photos remain in their own Google account instead of being stored on our server. We can also connect multiple Drive accounts."

---

## Q2. How does your search work without calling Gemini every time?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, we use Gemini when the photo is uploaded to understand the image and create its vector. We save that vector. During search, we compare the stored vectors locally, so we don't need to call Gemini for every search."

---

## Q3. What is a vector?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, a vector is a mathematical representation of the meaning of an image or text. Similar meanings have similar vectors, which allows us to compare images and searches."

---

## Q4. What is Cosine Similarity?

**Answer:**

> "Cosine Similarity measures how similar two vectors are. A higher value means the two vectors are more similar."

---

## Q5. What happens when the first Google Drive becomes full?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, our system checks the available storage before uploading. If the first Drive does not have enough space, it automatically tries the next connected Drive."

---

## Q6. Can PixSort access all files in the user's Google Drive?

**Answer:**

> "No Sir. We use the `drive.file` permission, which limits the application's access to files created or managed by PixSort."

---

## Q7. How do you keep different users' photos separate?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, every user has a unique Firebase user ID. We store this ID with the photo information and use Firestore Security Rules to make sure users can only access their own data."

---

## Q8. Why did you use React?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, React makes it easier to build a dynamic interface with reusable components. It is also suitable for applications where the UI changes frequently based on user actions."

---

## Q9. What is the main advantage of PixSort?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, the main advantage is that users can search their photos using normal language instead of remembering file names or manually organizing photos."

---

## Q10. What happens if a user disconnects a Google Drive account?

**Answer:**

> "Sir, PixSort removes that account from its storage pool. The original files in the user's Google Drive are not deleted."
